## How the technology works

VTEC changes cam profiles, allowing different valve timing and lift characteristics across operating conditions. Its purpose is to reconcile useful low/mid-speed behaviour with high-speed performance. Hydraulic pins connect rocker arms so the high-lift profile can operate the valves. ECU decisions depend on operating conditions; one universal engagement RPM would be misleading (Honda Motor Co., Ltd., 2025; S2).

In the 2000 DOHC i-VTEC design, VTC adds continuous adjustment of intake timing. Honda presented fuel economy, emissions and performance as joint objectives. This is evidence of the technology's intended benefits, not proof that every later i-VTEC engine has identical hardware or consumption (Honda Motor Co., Ltd., 2000; S3).

## FD2 case-study facts

Honda's 2007 release describes a naturally aspirated K20A 2.0-litre engine, rated at 165 kW (225 PS) at 8,000 RPM and 215 Nm at 6,100 RPM, with a six-speed manual transmission. Intake/exhaust improvements, higher compression and throttle control contributed alongside i-VTEC. Therefore, attributing the entire output to VTEC alone would be inaccurate (Honda Motor Co., Ltd., 2007; S5).

## Critical interpretation and poster plan

The story is about adapting valve operation to different needs, followed by improvements and wider applications. A production car is not itself a prototype, and FD2 is not the first VTEC vehicle. Manufacturer sources offer strong dates and specifications but are promotional accounts; they do not independently quantify society-wide benefits.
